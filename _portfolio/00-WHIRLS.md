From launching my first-time ballon radiosonde to analysing ocean profiles as they came in, July aboard R/V Marion Dufresne in the **WHIRLS** cruises was an experience I will not forget.

From June 27 - July 29, 2026, I had the opportunity to participate in the [WHIRLS 2026 expedition](https://www.whirls.eu/expedition/) in the Cape Basin of the South Atlantic. This five-week, dual-vessel field campaign brought together scientists from across Europe and South Africa aboard R/V Marion Dufresne and S.A. Agulhas II.

WHIRLS aims to understand how fine-scale ocean structures—including eddies, fronts and filaments—shape air-sea exchange, marine biogeochemistry, biodiversity and, ultimately, our climate.

My work on board including ocean CTD sampling, data analysis and science communication:

🧪 I participated in biogeochemical water sampling from the CTD rosette, focusing particularly on **salinity and dissolved oxygen samples**. 

⚗️ I learned how shipboard **salinometer measurements and dissolved-oxygen titrations** are used to calibrate and validate oceanographic sensors. 

📊 I contributed to the **preliminary processing, quality control and analysis of temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen and nitrate profiles collected by the CTD and Moving Vessel Profiler (MVP300).** This included helping turn newly collected observations into vertical sections across eddies and ocean fronts.

🎈 I launched **my first weather balloon** carrying a radiosonde—definitely one of the most memorable “firsts” of the expedition.

🎙️ I **organised and chaired an onboard WHIRLS science and outreach seminar**, where I also gave a talk entitled “**Why Ocean Observations Matter for Understanding Climate Change.**”

And between sampling shifts, data processing and scientific discussions, I had the chance to experience the South Atlantic at its most spectacular: remarkable sunsets, rainbows stretching across the ocean, and whales appearing beside the ship.

Over the past 5 years, I have processed millions of in-situ profiles for my study on ocean climate change, and with this cruise voyage, this is my first time seeing how these millions of profiles I dealt with over the past 5 years are collected, sampled, and calibrated!

I am grateful to the entire WHIRLS team and the crew of R/V Marion Dufresne for the science, teamwork and unforgettable moments at sea.
