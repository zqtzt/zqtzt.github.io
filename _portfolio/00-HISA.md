🌍 **Beyond Climate Science: Connecting Knowledge, Policy, and Action**

**On October 15-18, 2026,** I had the honor to join the **Global Youth Festival 2026**, organised by the Headway Institute of Strategic Alliance (HISA) in Paris, as a **Funded Delegate**.

One of the most memorable experiences was visiting the **French National Assembly (Assemblée nationale)**, which offered a valuable opportunity to reflect on how public policies are shaped and how scientific evidence can inform decision-making.

Throughout the festival, discussions ranged from global governance and diplomacy to well-being, the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), responsible media communication, and the role of science and innovation in addressing global challenges.

Coming from a background in climate change and ocean science, I found these cross-disciplinary perspectives particularly meaningful.

Much of my research focuses on understanding how the ocean is changing under climate change. Yet understanding these changes is only part of the challenge. An equally important question is how scientific knowledge can help societies respond effectively.

This is especially relevant to **international ocean governance**, where protecting marine ecosystems requires not only scientific evidence, but also international cooperation, policy coordination, and long-term planning. These considerations are increasingly shaping my interests in climate-resilient marine conservation, particularly under the BBNJ Agreement.

What I appreciated most about this experience was the opportunity to step outside the academic environment, exchange perspectives with young people from different backgrounds, and see global challenges through a broader lens.

**Science helps us understand a changing world. Dialogue and cooperation help us decide how to respond.**
